Uniqueness and non-uniqueness pairs for the fractional Laplacian

If a function vanishes on a and its vanishes on another discrete set, does the function necessarily vanish identically?

\(f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^d),\quad f|_{\Lambda}=0,\quad (-\Delta)^sf|_{M}=0\quad\overset{?}{\Longrightarrow}\quad f\equiv0\)

What the paper adds The paper proves that \(f\) vanishes identically when its zeros have positive density at the logarithmic scale and those of \((-\Delta)^s f\) have positive density at a coarser scale. It also constructs nonzero functions when both zero sets are slightly sparser, which shows that the logarithmic threshold is .

In plain words

Here \(\Lambda\) and \(M\) are discrete subsets of \(\mathbb R^d\), that is, sets without accumulation points, and \((-\Delta)^s\) is the nonlocal operator given by the Fourier multiplier \(|\xi|^{2s}\). For \(0<s<1\), a nonzero \(f\) and \((-\Delta)^sf\) cannot both vanish on the same open set, whereas a nonzero function and its Fourier transform can vanish on a common open set. The question is how much of this rigidity remains when the zeros are known only on discrete sets.

The results

Theorem 1.1 (i) UPUniqueness at the logarithmic scale

In every dimension and for \(0<s<1\), if a function \(f\in X_{s,\sigma}(\mathbb R^d)\) vanishes on a set with positive density at the scale of \(\{c_1\log|k|\,\frac{k}{|k|}\}\) and \((-\Delta)^sf\) vanishes on a set with positive density at the scale of \(\{c_2(\log|k|)^\beta\frac{k}{|k|}\}\), with \(\beta>1\), then \(f\equiv0\).

Statement
Theorem 1.1.
Let \(\Hm=\Sp(\R^d)\), \(0<\alpha,s<1\), and \(\Lambda,M\subset \mathbb{R}^d\) be discrete. Set \(\beta = \alpha^{-1}\). Then \((\Lambda, M)\) is a \(\UP\) for \(\Hm\) in each of the following cases:
  1. (i)
    \(d\ge 1\), and there exist \(c_1,c_2>0\) such that \(\D^-_{\Phi_{1,c_1}}(\Lambda)>0\) and \(\D^-_{\Phi_{\beta,c_2}}(M)>0\).

Theorem 1.1 (ii) UPPower-scale zeros on the line

On the line, uniqueness persists when the zeros of \((-\Delta)^sf\) are only as dense as the power set \(\{\pm n^\alpha\}\), \(0<\alpha<1\), provided \(f\) vanishes on a set with positive density at the logarithmic scale.

Statement
Theorem 1.1.
Let \(\Hm=\Sp(\R^d)\), \(0<\alpha,s<1\), and \(\Lambda,M\subset \mathbb{R}^d\) be discrete. Set \(\beta = \alpha^{-1}\). Then \((\Lambda, M)\) is a \(\UP\) for \(\Hm\) in each of the following cases:
  1. (ii)
    \(d=1\), and there exist \(c>0\) such that \(\D^-_{\Phi_{1,c}}(\Lambda)>0\) and \(\D^-_{G_{\alpha}}(M)>0\).

Theorem 1.2 NUPNon-uniqueness for separated sets

If each point \(\gamma\) of either set is at distance at least \(Ae^{-B|\gamma|^\rho}\) from the other points of that set, for some \(\rho<1\), then infinitely many linearly independent functions vanish on \(\Lambda\) while their fractional Laplacians vanish on \(M\), even with subexponential decay.

Statement
Theorem 1.2. Let \(0<\rho<1\), \(s>0\), and \(0<p,q<1\). Let \(\Lambda, M\subset\R^d\) be \(\rho\)-separated satisfying (1.4). Then \(\{f\in\mathcal S_s^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^d) \st f|_\Lambda=0 \text{ and } (-\Delta)^s f|_M=0\}\) is infinite-dimensional. In particular, \((\Lambda,M)\) is a \(\NUP\) for \(\mathcal S_s^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^d)\).

Theorem 1.3 NUP, entireBand-limited counterexamples on lattices

For every \(s>0\), there is a nonzero Schwartz function with compactly supported Fourier transform that vanishes, together with its fractional Laplacian, on a given lattice \(M\mathbb Z^d\).

Statement
Theorem 1.3. Let \(s>0\), \(M\in \GL_d(\R)\) and \(\Hm = \{f \in \Sp(\R^d) \st \widehat{f} \in C_c^\infty(\R^d)\}\). Then \((M\Z^d,M\Z^d)\) is a \(\NUP\) for \(\Hm\).

Theorem 1.4 NUP, real-analyticReal-analytic counterexamples on the line

On the line and for \(s=\frac12\), any two sets with finite upper density at the power scale, such as \(\{\pm n^\alpha\}\), admit a nonzero real-analytic counterexample with subexponential decay.

Statement
Theorem 1.4. Let \(0<\alpha, p,q <1\) and let \(\Lambda,M\subset \R\) be discrete. If both \(\D^+_{G_\alpha}(\Lambda)\) and \(\D^+_{G_\alpha}(M)\) are finite, then \((\Lambda, M)\) is a \(\mathsf{NUP}_{\frac{1}{2}}\) for \(\mathcal{S}_{1/2}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R})\cap \mathscr{H}(\R)\).

The reading, one idea at a time

  1. 1The questionThe paper studies which pairs of discrete sets force a function to vanish identically when it vanishes on the first set and its fractional Laplacian vanishes on the second.
  2. 2A logarithmic thresholdZeros of \(f\) at the logarithmic scale, together with zeros of \((-\Delta)^sf\) at a power \(\beta>1\) of that scale, force \(f\equiv0\), whereas zeros of both at powers above \(1\) do not.
  3. 3Uniqueness pairsThe zeros give decay, the decay gives regularity of \(\widehat f\), and since \(|\xi|^{2s}\) is not smooth at the origin, it follows that \(\widehat f\equiv0\).
  4. 4Non-uniqueness pairsOn \(\rho\)-separated sets the values of \(f\) and of \((-\Delta)^sf\) can be prescribed independently, by summing translated copies of two fixed functions dilated to very small scales.
  5. 5Counterexamples on latticesOn a lattice, the samples depend only on periodized functions built from \(\widehat f\), and two linear conditions on three Fourier values leave enough freedom for a band-limited counterexample.
  6. 6Real-analytic counterexamplesWhen \(\widehat f\) is supported in \([0,\infty)\) the half-Laplacian is a derivative, and de Branges spaces supply such functions with double zeros at the power scale.
  7. 7The formalizationA Lean 4 formalization verifies the main theorems, and the published results it relies on enter only as explicit hypotheses.

Fourier interpolation on the line

Background (cited results) with a numerical illustration (\(\alpha=1/4\))

Λ\(\mathbb Z_\alpha=\{\pm n^\alpha\}\)
M\(\mathbb Z_\alpha\)
Parameters\(T=\mathcal F\)

What to notice. For the Fourier transform the threshold on power sets is \(\alpha=\tfrac12\). Uniqueness holds for \(\alpha<1-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\) by Ramos and Sousa and for \(\alpha<\tfrac12\) by Kulikov, Nazarov and Sodin, who also prove non-uniqueness for \(\alpha>\tfrac12\). Logarithmic lattices

Special cases of Theorem 1.1, drawn to scale (all sites up to radius 3.8)

Λ\(\Lambda^2_1(1)\) and \(\Lambda^1_1(1)=\{\pm\log n\}\)
M\(\Lambda^2_2(1)\), \(\Lambda^1_2(1)\) and \(\mathbb Z_\alpha\) with \(0<\alpha<1\)
Parameters\(0<s<1\), \(c_1=c_2=1\)

What to notice. The preimage of a ball of radius \(R\) under \(\Phi_{1,1}\) has radius \(e^R\), and under \(\Phi_{2,1}\) it has radius \(e^{\sqrt R}\). Decay from the zeros

Special cases of Propositions 3.2 and 3.4 on the model sets

Λ\(\Lambda^d_1(c_1)\)
M\(\Lambda^d_\beta(c_2)\) with \(\beta>1\), or \(\mathbb Z_\alpha\) with \(\tau=\alpha^{-1}-1\)
Parameters\(f\in\mathcal S\), \(g=(-\Delta)^sf\)

What to notice. Each zero set gives a decay rate at its own scale, namely exponential decay for logarithmic zeros, \(e^{-b|x|^{1/\beta}}\) for powers of the logarithm, and \(|x|^{-k\tau}\) for every \(k\) on \(\mathbb Z_\alpha\).
